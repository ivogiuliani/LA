Editorial Standards

Last updated: October 13, 2026

The Journal exists to show, with sources, what a luxury home in Southern California actually costs to build, insure and keep. These are the rules every note is held to. If a note falls short of them, write to us and we will fix it.

1. Sources

Every figure in a note links to the document it comes from: the page of the report, the rate filing, the press release or the article, never a publication's homepage. Each note names its primary source in the opening paragraph and lists every source it draws on at the end.

We prefer primary sources in this order:

  • Regulators and public bodies — California Department of Insurance, CAL FIRE and the Office of the State Fire Marshal, the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety, Los Angeles County, the Office of the State Insurance Commissioner's rate filings.
  • Research and standards bodies — the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS), the California FAIR Plan, academic studies.
  • Trade and general press — Insurance Journal, The Real Deal, the Los Angeles Times, Robb Report, Architectural Digest and similar, used for market facts and reporting that regulators do not publish.

Before a note is published, every external link is checked automatically; a link that does not resolve is removed or replaced, not left in place. We do not cite a source we could not open.

2. Dates and updates

Each note shows the date it was published and the date it was last changed, both on the page and in its structured data. The Westside Rebuild Tracker is rebuilt every Monday from Los Angeles open data; the Insurable Home in California page is regenerated whenever one of the notes it draws on changes, so its figures are never older than the newest note behind them.

3. How notes are produced

The Journal monitors insurance, fire-code, construction and market sources every day with software of our own. Drafting uses AI models working from the monitored source and from My Villa's own documented positions; a draft that cannot be anchored to a verified source is not published. The editorial desk is responsible for every note that appears, for its sources and for correcting it.

When two notes cover the same development, one of them is marked as a duplicate for search engines and kept online as a cited source, so the figures it carries remain traceable.

4. Reporting and opinion

Notes report what a source says and what the figures imply for building in Los Angeles. My Villa's own view appears once per note, in the block labelled Our Perspective, in the first person. Anything outside that block is reporting and analysis, not advocacy.

5. Our interests, declared

My Villa designs and delivers reinforced-concrete villas in Los Angeles. The Journal argues, with evidence, that construction material is the most consequential variable in a California home's cost, insurability and value. Readers should weigh that interest. We do not accept sponsored content, paid placements or payment from the sources we cite, and no source is shown a note before publication.

6. Corrections

If a figure, a quotation or an attribution is wrong, write to info@myvilla.la with the link to the note and the source that contradicts it. We verify against the source, correct the note and update its last-changed date. A material correction is noted at the end of the note.

7. Images and credits

Every photograph carries a credit naming the photographer and the publication it appeared in, or the Unsplash contributor. Renders, plans and diagrams of My Villa houses are our own work and are marked as such.

8. What the Journal is not

The Journal is not insurance, legal, tax or investment advice. Premium, discount and appraisal figures are the ones reported by the sources cited on the date shown; they vary by insurer, parcel and policy, and they change. Before making a decision about a property, verify the current figure with a licensed broker, an insurer or a lawyer admitted in California.
