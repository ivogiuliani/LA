Definition

An insurable home in California is a house whose structure and parcel meet the wildfire-mitigation standards that insurers are required to recognise and price, so that an admitted carrier can write it at a discounted rate instead of pushing it to the FAIR Plan. In 2026 that means the Safer from Wildfires measures as a floor and the IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home designation as the reference for the largest discounts.

How insurers look at a concrete home

IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home

The Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety designation has 2 tiers, Home and Home Plus, and it is the aggregate standard California carriers point to when they price mitigation. A home certified at the Plus level has been offered discounts of up to 50% by admitted carriers. A reinforced concrete envelope satisfies the structural items of the checklist by construction, without add-on assemblies; the parcel items (Zone 0, vents, decks) still have to be documented. Source: Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety · My Villa Journal, April 19, 2026

Safer from Wildfires and the Department of Insurance

Since the Department's regulation took effect, every admitted carrier must recognise 12 hardening measures organised in 3 layers layers, structure, parcel and community, and reflect them in rates. For an underwriter, a concrete home is a structure-layer answer to most of the list at once: non-combustible walls, Class A roof, ember-resistant vents and enclosed eaves are design decisions, not retrofits. What remains is the parcel layer, which is where most applications fail. Source: Insurance Journal · My Villa Journal, April 19, 2026

FAIR Plan

The FAIR Plan now covers up to $20M per building, which makes it a real fallback for a high-value home, and its 29.1% rate increase in October 2026 makes it an expensive one. Its own wildfire discount stack tops out at 16.4%, roughly a third of what an IBHS-certified home has been offered in the admitted market. The gap between the two numbers is the financial case for building to the standard. Source: Nate Wittasek, P.E. (Substack) · My Villa Journal, June 1, 2026

Carrier capacity

State Farm reported $7.6B in insured losses from the January 2025 Los Angeles fires and, as of 2026, has kept new homeowner business paused. Capacity, not only price, is the constraint: a documented, certifiable home is the submission an underwriter can say yes to. Source: Latent Insurance · My Villa Journal, June 4, 2026

What is actually being built

Our Westside Rebuild Tracker, rebuilt weekly from City of Los Angeles permit data, shows that of the 2,494 new-home permits filed since January 2025 that declare a construction type, only 11 (0.4%) are Type I or II, the non-combustible category. The rest are wood-frame. Source: LADBS open data, retrieved 2026-09-16.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a home "insurable" in California in 2026?

An insurable home is one whose construction and parcel meet the mitigation standards insurers are required to price: the 12 Safer from Wildfires measures recognised by the California Department of Insurance, and, for the largest discounts, the IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home designation, which admitted carriers have rewarded with premium reductions of up to 50%.

How much can a hardened home save on wildfire insurance?

Two documented figures exist. On a FAIR Plan policy the wildfire-discount stack reaches 16.4%. With an IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home designation, admitted carriers have offered discounts of up to 50%. Both depend on the carrier, the parcel and the documentation submitted; treat them as ranges, not quotes.

Does building to the Wildfire Prepared Home Plus standard cost much more?

Headwaters Economics and IBHS estimate a construction-cost premium of about 3% for a home built to the Wildfire Prepared Home Plus level compared with a conventional build.

What is the California FAIR Plan and what changed for high-value homes?

The FAIR Plan is the state's insurer of last resort. In 2025 its coverage limit was raised to $20M per building, and a 29.1% rate increase takes effect on October 15, 2026, the largest in the plan's history. For an owner of a high-value home it is a fallback, priced accordingly, not a strategy.

Is State Farm writing new home policies in California?

As of 2026, State Farm, the state's largest home insurer, has kept new homeowner business paused, after an emergency 17% rate increase approved by the Department of Insurance in June 2025.

What is Zone 0?

Zone 0 is the 5 ft ember-resistant perimeter immediately around the structure, the first zone IBHS and CAL FIRE look at. California's Board of Forestry and Fire Protection is codifying it as a non-combustible strip with no vegetation, mulch or wood fencing against the walls.
